# Publish the fabric stories educators skipped

The best LinkedIn material in "AI networking" as of 2 October 2026 sits at two seams that no mass-audience educator has crossed: the fabric between GPUs (scale-up, scale-out, scale-across and, since 29 September, what Nvidia calls "scale-in") and the network behaviour of agent protocols and agent memory. Between April and October 2026 Karpathy, Ng, Vergadia and Donner published almost entirely on the software layer of agents, and Vergadia's own GPU explainer stops at a single GPU with no mention of NVLink, Ethernet, RDMA or optics ([thecloudgirl.dev](https://www.thecloudgirl.dev/how-nvidia-gpus-power-the-ai-revolution/)), while the fabric moved fast: **AI back-end switch spending overtook the entire front-end data-centre switch market in Q2 2026** ([Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/news/ai-back-end-networks-switch-sales-surpass-front-end-networks-for-the-first-time-in-2q2026/)), UALink shipped a 2.0 specification before any 1.0 silicon existed ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/07/ualink_2_specs/)), Nvidia's co-packaged-optics Ethernet switches entered full production ([StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/nvidia-spectrum-x-ethernet-photonics-enters-full-production-with-4x-fewer-lasers-and-a-five-vendor-cpo-supply-chain)) and MCP was rebuilt to run stateless behind a plain round-robin load balancer ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/)). The top three topics are a four-plane visual glossary ("scale-up, scale-out, scale-across, scale-in") timed to OCP Global Summit on 12-15 October; "your agent's memory is now a network hop", which connects Karpathy's "context window" framing to BlueField-4, CXL and smart fabrics; and "why MCP dropped sessions", the only topic with measured search demand (**"model context protocol" at about 40.5K monthly searches**, flagged as past peak) ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/model-context-protocol)). The people are quotable on software; only vendors are quotable on fabrics. That asymmetry is the opening.

### 3. Why MCP dropped sessions: the load balancer won

The 28 July 2026 MCP specification (release candidate 21 May, 10-week validation window) is the most network-relevant change in agent tooling this year: the protocol became stateless, removing the `initialize`/`initialized` handshake and the `Mcp-Session-Id` header, moving client metadata into `_meta` on each request and adding `server/discover`, with the stated aim that servers can "run behind a plain round-robin load balancer" without sticky sessions; Streamable HTTP now requires `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers so "load balancers, gateways, and rate-limiters" can route without parsing the body; response caching is formalised with `ttlMs` and `cacheScope`; six SEPs align authorisation with OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect; Logging is deprecated in favour of OpenTelemetry ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/)). The same month, IETF 126 in Vienna (18-24 July) reached consensus to charter a working group only for agent discovery (DAWN), while the broader agentproto BoF on MCP, A2A, ACP and ANP ended in continued mailing-list discussion ([IETF 126 recap](https://www.ietf.org/blog/ietf126-recap/)); a consultancy blog reports A2A joining the Agentic AI Foundation on 17 August and AAIF membership reaching 247 organisations by 13 August, neither confirmed against a Linux Foundation release ([Practical Logix](https://www.practicallogix.com/the-2026-agent-interoperability-reset-a2a-mcp-under-aaif/)). Demand is the strongest of any topic: **"model context protocol" draws about 40.5K monthly searches, up 4,400% over two years, with status "Peaked"** ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/model-context-protocol)), which is precisely why a "what is MCP" post is dead and a "what MCP did to your load balancer" post is alive. For GEO, MCP is a heavily cited subject, so the headline rule matters most here: articles whose headline contained every query word ranked 42.1% of the time versus 7.2% with low coverage ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/linkedin-seo-5-steps-from-1-260-searches-and-9-805-urls-319753c72c)). The coverage gap is that every explanation of the spec is developer-facing; the agentic researcher calls it "a clear, under-explained protocol meets infrastructure story". It builds on Donner's AI Coder course, which teaches MCP alongside hooks and subagents but nothing underneath it, on DeepLearning.AI's "MCP: Build Rich-Context AI Apps with Anthropic" ([course](https://learn.deeplearning.ai/courses/mcp-build-rich-context-ai-apps-with-anthropic)), on IBM's "A2A vs MCP" format, and on the modest but real appetite for MCP internals shown by the World's Fair talks "Why MCP and ChatGPT Apps Use Double Iframes" (7,882 views) and "MCP Apps: Primitives, Discovery, and the Future of Software" (4,055 views) ([Lawrence Wu](https://lawrencewu.net/posts/2026-07-09-aie-2026-youtube-popularity/)). The general GEO traits come from older web-page experiments: adding statistics raised visibility by about 37-41% and quotations by 28-40% in the 2024 Princeton/Georgia Tech paper, 55% of AI Overview citations come from the top 30% of a page, 76.4% of ChatGPT citations were under 30 days old in 2025, original research earns 4.31x the citations of directory content, YouTube presence is the strongest correlate of AI Overview visibility at about 23.3% of citations, 62% of AI citations come from outside Google's top ten, and llms.txt showed zero correlation with citations ([Radiant Elephant](https://www.radiantelephant.com/?p=47908)). Ahrefs' 2026 headlines, read as titles only, add that AI Overviews appear for 83% of branded searches and that a 331,000-page study found Google does not penalise AI-written content as such ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-trends/)). The practical reading is that a LinkedIn article with the exact query in its headline, a definition and dated numbers in the first paragraph and a named quotation still earns citations, but mainly from Google's surfaces and Perplexity, and that the same explainer should be mirrored on YouTube and an owned domain because LinkedIn's share proved volatile within six months.

For Google ranking proper, an August 2026 analysis of 1,260 searches and 9,805 LinkedIn URLs found Pulse articles in Google's top 20 for 46.5% of tested searches, in 30% of AI Overviews and 41% of Perplexity results but only 1.5% of ChatGPT and Claude answers; **feed posts without backlinks ranked top 20 only 6.7% of the time versus 31.2% with backlinks**, the median ranking feed post was 300 days old, articles whose headline contained every query word ranked 42.1% of the time against 7.2% with under a third of the words, query words in the first 50 characters beat characters 50-150 (29.1% vs 16.3%), and word count, emoji and heading count did not predict ranking ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/linkedin-seo-5-steps-from-1-260-searches-and-9-805-urls-319753c72c)). A conflicting secondhand figure attributes to Foundation Inc. an 89% collapse in Pulse organic traffic alongside a 250% rise for standard posts ([Neal Schaffer](https://newsletter.nealschaffer.com/posts/linkedin-pulse-lost-89-of-its-google-traffic)); the two reconcile if articles rank well for the specific queries they target while aggregate Pulse traffic fell, and either way a feed post alone rarely ranks unless something links to it, and not for months.

## Carousels beat video, personal beats company, 150-175 words wins reach

The three large 2026 datasets agree on packaging. Socialinsider's 1.3M posts from 16,645 business pages put 2025 engagement rates at **native documents 7.00%, multi-image 6.45%, video 6.00%, image 5.30%, text 4.50%, poll 4.20% and link 3.25%**, with average video views down 36% year on year even as pages doubled video output ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-benchmark/)); Metricool's 673,658 posts found personal profiles earning 63% more engagement than company pages, carousels and multi-image posts beating standard formats, video now the most-used format on personal profiles yet underperforming carousels, and visible reactions falling (likes -13%, comments -17%) while clicks rose 5% ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-linkedin-study-2026/)); DSMN8's 110,000 employee posts concluded "no single format wins across every metric" ([DSMN8](https://dsmn8.com/resources/whats-working-on-linkedin-in-2026-report/)). On length, The Shield Index's 167,940 posts show engagement rising slightly with length but **reach peaking at 150-175 words**, the reach advantage of very long posts shrinking from 1.77x to 1.32x between November 2025 and February 2026, length helping text-only posts (+84% reach from short to very long) but hurting document posts, moderate line breaks (2-5 per 100 words) beating the one-sentence-per-line "airy" template, and accounts under 25K followers peaking at 150-250 words ([The Shield Index](https://theshieldindex.beehiiv.com/p/does-linkedin-post-length-actually-matter)). On cadence, Buffer's 2025 regression over 2M posts found that moving from one post a week to 2-5 adds about 1,182 impressions per post ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)), and SignalScout's smaller B2B creator sample shows a power-law distribution (mean 58 engagements, top 10% at 326+, top 1% at 2,000+) in which over 90% of engagers on any post are first-time engagers and early discussion beats passive likes ([Koka Sexton](https://kokasexton.com/linkedin-algorithm-report-2026/)). Several widely repeated numbers are vendor claims without a traceable source and should be treated that way: Dataslayer's "external links cut reach by about 60%", "posts are tested on 2-5% of the network", "first 60 minutes are decisive", "5-10 slides" and "30-90 seconds" ([Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)); the "carousels get 278% more engagement than video" figure appears on Buffer's page and as Dataslayer's own testing, an attribution conflict that makes it a single unverified data point; and the "11x the interactions of images" headline from a French summary of Metricool was not verified in the primary. The direction of the link penalty is supported by link posts having the lowest measured engagement rate, so the YouTube link belongs in a comment or in the article, never as the post itself.

Video should be chosen for what it shows, not for reach. Supply is up and views per video are down, LinkedIn ad benchmarks relayed secondhand put median watch time at 5.86 seconds with 60.5% of viewers not completing ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/linkedin-video-statistics/)), and 91% of video is reportedly watched muted, which makes burned-in captions mandatory and means the key diagram must be on screen in the first three seconds. That is why every video concept above is a demo, a trace or an animation rather than a talking head, and why each pairs with a carousel that carries the same diagram one idea per slide with a short caption, because long captions depress carousel reach. The split that follows from the evidence is a full explainer on YouTube for Google and AI Overview visibility, a native 30-60 second cut plus a carousel on the personal profile, a LinkedIn article with the query-matching headline for search and citation, and a mirror on an owned domain, with 2-5 posts a week sustained rather than one polished post a month.

Human voice is now measured against the author rather than for them. A detector vendor classified **81.2% of 5,000 LinkedIn posts over 100 words as "Likely AI" in July 2026**, up from about 50% in late 2024, and reports LinkedIn's Chief Product Officer saying the platform is ramping a "Seems like AI slop" member feedback option; classifiers have false positives and the study measured no engagement effect ([Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement)). No measured evidence shows AI-sounding posts losing reach, but the practical markers of a human author are things a model cannot supply: named hardware and configurations, the author's own measurements or failure stories, a stated opinion about a vendor claim, plain paragraphs rather than one-line stanzas, and a closing question about a real design trade-off, which also fits the finding that early discussion outperforms likes. Every topic above has been written with a built-in disagreement (a vendor number to doubt, two surveys that conflict, a spec that beat its own silicon) precisely so that the post can carry a point of view.

## Conclusion

The defensible position for this author is translator rather than reporter. Two literate communities now stop at the same wall from opposite sides: agent builders, taught by Karpathy, Ng, Donner and Vergadia, treat the model's API as the edge of the system, and network engineers, served by Bombal and Packet Pushers, treat the fabric as something agents operate rather than something agents depend on. Every topic ranked here sits on that wall, and the human-organisation metaphor that dominates agent explainers leaves an entire network metaphor (addressing, discovery, routing, congestion, trust boundaries) unclaimed; the IETF's decision to charter discovery first, Nvidia's "scale-in" and MCP's header-routable redesign all say the infrastructure world is already thinking in those terms, which means the vocabulary will exist whether or not an educator supplies it.

Two implications follow for how to publish. First, the keyword gap is a task, not a finding: the networking queries have never been measured in these notes, and a short Ahrefs or Trends pull will decide whether topics 1, 4, 5 and 6 are low-volume high-intent or simply low-volume. Second, the LinkedIn GEO channel is decaying faster than the LinkedIn engagement channel, with ChatGPT at 0.06% citation of LinkedIn articles while Google's surfaces still cite them, so the durable asset is the explainer on YouTube and an owned domain, with LinkedIn as the discussion surface that feeds backlinks into it. The next six weeks are unusually generous: OCP hands over the vocabulary in mid-October, SC26 in mid-November is when Q4's promised silicon either appears or does not, and the author who has a four-plane diagram, a memory ladder and a spec-versus-silicon timeline already published will be the one the trade press and the answer engines have to cite.
