Trust and data

What happens to a client's material

Every client works in a workspace of their own. This page says who can see it, where it travels, and when it is deleted.

i.

Separation and access

One workspace per client

Each client's articles, research, voice samples and settings live in their own workspace. People see only the workspaces they were added to, and files are only ever served from the workspace they belong to.

Personal accounts and roles

Everyone signs in with their own account. A role per workspace decides what they can do: owners manage it, editors approve and publish, writers draft, and client reviewers read, comment and sign off without ever starting a paid run.

Passwords and sessions

Passwords are stored only as salted hashes. Repeated failed sign-ins from one address are locked out for five minutes. Changing a password or disabling an account ends that person's other sessions.

Approval before anything goes out

A workspace can require an editor or the client to approve an article before it is published. An article edited after approval goes back for approval, and a client's public feed carries only what was approved.

ii.

Credentials and records

WordPress access

Publishing uses a WordPress application password, which the client can revoke from their own WordPress at any time. It is stored encrypted, and never shown again once saved.

Audit log

Sign-ins, failed sign-ins, every run, every status change, every edit, every publish and every settings change is recorded with who, when and from which address.

Retention

Each workspace can set how many days finished work is kept; older articles, their versions, discussion and logs are then deleted. Drafts uploaded for evaluation are deleted after two days regardless.

In transit

The app is served over HTTPS only{% if hosted %}, with HSTS{% endif %}. A form or script on another site cannot act with a signed-in browser's session.
